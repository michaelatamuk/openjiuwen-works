# Two Mondays — 1:55 cut

**Length:** 1:55
**Format:** 16:9 master, 4K, 25 or 30 fps · vertical 9:16 cut-down · silent captions
**Language:** English voice-over, English captions
**Goal:** in under two minutes, a cold viewer can retell the story and remember the product name

---

## The concept

One customer. One developer. One bug. The same Monday, twice.

Today it takes four days and ends with nobody telling the customer. Rewind: the same Monday takes one morning, and the last shot is the same invoice — now correct.

**The line the film must earn:**

> The waiting moved. The judgement stayed.

## Voice-over, clean copy

> Berlin, Monday, nine a.m. A customer opens an invoice: 21% VAT. It should be 19%.
>
> Today, that fix takes four days. A queue. An agent waiting for answers. A reviewer waiting for time.
>
> Almost none of it is writing code.
>
> Now the same Monday, again.
>
> Three ways in: a failure caught in the app, a bug already filed, a person reporting it.
>
> The engine reads the code, drafts a plan, and stops. The developer approves it — one click.
>
> It builds in its own sandbox. A second agent checks it, with tests the first never saw.
>
> No diff to read. One page: what changed, what was tested, what it cost.
>
> One more click. The fix ships.
>
> And it goes back where the bug was seen: the invoice is correct, the ticket closes, the customer gets a reply.
>
> Nothing was forked. jiuwenswarm builds, agent-core checks, PPMA remembers.
>
> openJiuwen Works. The waiting moved. The judgement stayed.

About 140 words — roughly 62 seconds of speech, which leaves 53 seconds for the timeline drawing, the two clicks, the proof page and the closing beat. The film breathes without being slow.

---

## What makes the 1:55 work

- **Four visual anchors carry the story**: 21%, the four-day ticker, the proof page, 19%. Everything else is connective tissue.
- **Silence at both clicks.** They are the loudest sounds in the film.
- **No second anecdote.** One customer, one bug, one loop.
- **The first and last frame are the same invoice.** That is the whole argument, told without a slide.

---

## Craft rules

**Camera and screen**
- Three surfaces: a phone, a laptop, the Works window. Hands and screens, no faces.
- Every UI moment is a real recording. If the product is not ready, use deck stills and label the film *concept*.

**Sound**
- One low sustained note under the first act, resolving after the rewind. Silence at the clicks. No music in the last five seconds.

**Type and colour**
- Inter. Grey-blue for today, purple for the second Monday. Captions burned in, two lines maximum.

**Avoid**
- Robots, glowing brains, code rain, “autonomous”, invented numbers beyond the two timelines.

---

## Production checklist

1. Invoice mockup in two states: 21% and 19%.
2. The four-day ticker as motion; a one-frame flash of each day.
3. Seeded task with a finished run and a proof page.
4. Two gate states: plan awaiting approval, release awaiting approval.
5. Three return shots: corrected invoice, closed ticket, customer reply.
6. Four product cards from the deck.
7. Room tone; one music stem.
8. Clean data: fictional customer, no real names, tokens or logos.

## The test of a good cut

Ask someone who has never seen the product to retell it. If they say **“it takes the fix back to the customer”**, it works. If they say **“it's an AI coding tool”**, it does not.
